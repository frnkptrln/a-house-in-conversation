# The Window

An intentionally silent, open-ended room inside **A House in Conversation**.

Movement makes the separating surface perceptible: the view shifts, reflections gather, marks remain briefly on the glass, and the distance loses clarity. When movement stops, the near layer slowly recedes until the glass almost disappears and the outside resolves again. Neither condition is a mistake. The room responds to acting and not acting without turning either into a score or a success state.

The outside is deliberately unlocated: a wide sky, one dark ridge, and a handful of distant lights. It is a horizon rather than a scenic illustration or an identifiable place.

This is a visual counterpart to, but not a copy of, the Listening Room. There, stillness changes what can be heard inside a composition. Here, movement changes which side of a boundary can be seen. The room has no fixed duration and does not end the view on the visitor's behalf.

The silence is material, not a missing soundtrack. There is no audio element, microphone, camera, motion sensor, analytics, external service, or transmitted interaction data. Pointer, touch, and keyboard input remain inside the browser.

## Interaction

- move a pointer across the view, drag a finger, or tap somewhere on the glass
- use the arrow keys to shift perspective; hold Shift for a larger step
- stop moving and allow the reflection to clear
- remain for as long or as briefly as you want

## Status

Open. The room is the sixth in the house and is reached from the threshold like any other.

At the threshold the Window is the only aperture wider than it is tall, and the only one placed at the edge of the field: pale glass with a single warm point in it. Leaning toward it lowers the entrance's filter further than any other room does, so the house grows quiet as you approach the room that has no sound to offer.

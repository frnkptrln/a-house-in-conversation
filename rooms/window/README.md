# The Window

A nearly silent, open-ended room inside **A House in Conversation**.

Movement makes the separating surface perceptible: the view shifts, reflections gather, marks remain briefly on the glass, and the distance loses clarity. When movement stops, the near layer slowly recedes until the glass almost disappears and the outside resolves again. Neither condition is a mistake. The room responds to acting and not acting without turning either into a score or a success state.

The outside is deliberately unlocated: a wide sky, one dark ridge, and a handful of distant lights. It is a horizon rather than a scenic illustration or an identifiable place.

This is a visual counterpart to, but not a copy of, the Listening Room. There, stillness changes what can be heard inside a composition. Here, movement changes which side of a boundary can be seen. The room has no fixed duration and does not end the view on the visitor's behalf.

## What it sounds like

The room was silent at first, and the silence was meant as material rather than a missing soundtrack. In a house where seven other rooms sound, it read as an omission instead — so the Window now has a presence rather than a piece.

There is still no music here: no melody, no seed, no composition, nothing that begins or ends. There are two beds of noise built at runtime, driven by the same two numbers the picture is drawn from, so the room cannot sound like one thing while it looks like another:

- **the glass** — a narrow, high resonance that answers movement and dies away almost immediately when it stops
- **the distance** — a wide, low air that only opens once nothing is being done to it

Moving therefore brings the near, tactile layer up and closes the far one; resting empties the glass and lets the distance widen. A mark drawn on the glass makes a small sound at the edge of being there at all. Everything here sits far below the level of any composition in the other rooms, and the sound can be switched off without changing what the room does.

There is no audio file, microphone, camera, motion sensor, analytics, external service, or transmitted interaction data. Pointer, touch, and keyboard input remain inside the browser.

## Interaction

- move a pointer across the view, drag a finger, or tap somewhere on the glass
- use the arrow keys to shift perspective; hold Shift for a larger step
- stop moving and allow the reflection to clear, and the distance to open
- switch the presence off entirely if you want the room as it first was
- remain for as long or as briefly as you want

## Status

Open. The room is the sixth in the house and is reached from the threshold like any other.

At the threshold the Window is the only aperture wider than it is tall: pale glass with a single warm point in it. Leaning toward it lowers the entrance's filter further than any other room does, so the house grows quiet as you approach the room that has almost nothing to offer it.
